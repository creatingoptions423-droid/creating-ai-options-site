Our method

From requirement
to reviewable result.

A deliberate sequence for defining expectations, examining behavior, and documenting what the evidence supports.

  1. 01

    Intake

    Understand the software, intended users, review goals, available environments, and practical constraints. Agree the scope and access needed for testing.

  2. 02

    Requirements review

    Identify expected behavior and acceptance criteria. Surface ambiguity, missing prerequisites, and open questions before treating assumptions as requirements.

  3. 03

    Test design

    Create traceable test cases with preconditions, inputs, steps, and expected outcomes. Include edge cases and exploratory questions relevant to the scope.

  4. 04

    Controlled execution

    Run checks in the agreed environment. Record the build, test conditions, and observations so results remain grounded in the execution context.

  5. 05

    Evidence capture

    Associate relevant captures, logs, and notes with each test. Keep expected and observed behavior separate, and handle sensitive information according to agreed boundaries.

  6. 06

    Defect documentation

    Describe deviations precisely. Record affected workflows, supporting evidence, classification, and any uncertainty or blocker that limits the conclusion.

  7. 07

    Reproduction

    Repeat the documented path where possible. Identify the conditions needed to reproduce a failure and clearly flag behavior that cannot be reproduced consistently.

  8. 08

    Verification

    Retest a reported fix against the original expectation and record the new conditions. Check related behavior for regressions within the agreed scope.

  9. 09

    Final report

    Bring together scope, execution context, results, evidence references, defects, blockers, and untested areas. Separate verified behavior from unresolved questions.

Principles / Applied throughout

Evidence over assumption.

Evidence Over Assumption

A behavior is not verified because it appears correct.

Reproducibility

Failures should include enough context to reproduce them.

Structured Classification

PASS, FAIL, BLOCKED, N/A, and observations remain distinct.

Human Judgment

Automation supports evaluation. It does not replace judgment where behavior is ambiguous.
